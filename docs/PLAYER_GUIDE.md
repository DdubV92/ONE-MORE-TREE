# One More Tree: player guide

## Starting

- **Continue** loads your most recent unfinished campaign (the slot, its state and save time are shown under the button).
- **New Campaign** gives a short brief and asks for a slot. A slot that already has a save always asks before replacing it (the old save is kept once as a `.bak`).
- **Load** lists every slot with its state (contract day, survival day, completed, finished), logs delivered, cash, save time and any checkpoints.
- Before playing, open **Settings** to set text size, captions, horror intensity, flash reduction, head-bob, camera shake and key bindings.
- **World art style** (Settings): `drawn` is the hand-drawn pixel world (default). If distant detail shimmers on your screen, raise **Drawn world pixel rows**, or pick `anime` for the earlier smooth painted look.

## Your first day

A short guide in the top-right walks you through real work: walk, read the contract in the Journal, meet the crew and Biscuit, find the delivery rack, fell a tree, cut it into logs, carry one and deliver it (the money you see is the real payment), check wages, then get the night rules and sleep. Each step finishes when you actually do the thing, in any order and with any keys. **Journal → Help & log** lets you skip a step, skip the guide (you then get a one-page rules summary) or replay the hints. Replaying never changes your trees or money.

## The contract: three lots

- You sign for three marked lots of forest just outside the camp's forest entrance. Lot 1 has 30 trees (orange paint, due by day 10), lot 2 has 35 (blue, day 20) and lot 3 has 40 (yellow, day 30).
- A lot is finished when every painted tree is down, its stump is out and its logs are delivered. Journal → Contract shows each lot's progress.
- The company pays an advance when a lot opens ($3,000, $3,500, $4,000), the same again when it is finished, and a bonus ($1,500, $2,000, $2,500) if that is by its due day.
- Your crew fells painted trees only. You can cut any tree: wood from outside the lots is still paid at the sawmill, but it does not count toward the contract.
- Finish all three lots by nightfall on day 30 and the contract is won.

## Money and crew

- Wood is paid at the sawmill behind the camper: $48 for every pallet it finishes (1,000 litres of log, about $100 for an average tree). Felling alone pays nothing, and burned logs pay nothing.
- A delivered log counts toward the contract at once, wherever the camp takes it: the sawmill's feed rack (10 logs), the receiving cradle by the fire, or a log deck outside the north-east fence. If there's no room, the delivery is refused and the log stays with you.
- Your crew carries logs straight to the sawmill while its rack has room, and to the cradle when it is full. Workers on Auto, or anyone on **Yard** duty, move waiting logs from the cradle and decks to the mill. The saw stops taking new logs at dusk.
- In campaigns started before the sawmill, each log is still paid when the yard accepts it, and the fire burns what is on its loading pile.
- The handcart by the fire moves 4 logs at once, and logs too heavy to lift can be dragged.
- Each worker has their own daily rate, at least $100, set in the Payroll app on the camper computer, plus any bonuses; it is settled when you sleep. Pay below what a worker feels is fair makes them unhappy, and they may quit (always with at least one day's "on the verge" warning). Unpaid wages stay owed from their day; one more unpaid workday and that worker leaves. Paying part doesn't reset that.
- Example: two workers on $100 cost $200 a day. About two average trees through the sawmill earn that back.
- Axes: small ×1.00, medium ×1.40, large ×1.96 cutting rate. Hard-working: ×1.25. Stronger: carries more by hand. Timid: frightens sooner and heads home earlier.

## Starting a campaign: Nights

- **Cozy:** contract nights cannot catch you (the warnings still come) and the island stirs less. The hunt after the contract is unchanged.
- **Standard:** the game as designed.
- **Long Night:** the island stirs sooner, the dark closes in a quarter faster, and the hunt after a finished contract lasts a day longer.

## One more tree

- When all three lots are finished the company makes one last offer over the radio: $5,000 for the tallest tree past the forest entrance, if you fell it yourself within seven minutes (the crew has packed up). It gets a white band and shows on the map; the top of the screen counts the time down.
- Say yes and bring it down: you are paid, and the ship waits one more day for the timber, so the hunt lasts a day longer. Say yes and fail: nothing. Say no: the ordinary three days.
- The ending remembers what you answered.

## The map

- **Journal → Map** (the last tab) is your survey sheet. The coast is drawn in; the inside fills in as you walk it.
- It shows the camp, the sawmill and the dock, every lot tree in its paint colour (dark once it is down), the work lamps you have set down and your own arrow. "Around you" zooms in.
- If a worker has not come home it marks where they were last working. In the hunt it marks the beacon parts once you have checked the beacon.
- It never shows anything that moves at night.

## Animals

- Gulls stand on the shore, crabs on the sand, and a few deer graze in the far woods. Walk up and the gulls lift, the crabs run and the deer bolt. None of them can be harmed.
- They are out in daylight only. **When the gulls all lift and go out to sea, dusk has started: go home.**
- As the nights get worse there are fewer of them each morning. Journal → Field notes says what you are still seeing.

## Things to find

- Eight small things stand out in the far forest and along the shore: a stake, a fire ring, a sign. Walk up to one and choose "Look closer".
- Each gets one line in Journal → Field notes and a mark on the map. They are always in the same places on the same island.
- Nothing is explained. The ending counts how many you found.

## Your crew: belief and trust

- **Journal → Crew** says what each worker believes and how far they trust you, and what that means.
- **Belief:** someone convinced something is out there heads in early and stays steadier when it shows itself. A sceptic works later into the dusk and takes a real sighting hard.
- **Trust:** someone who trusts you takes a risky order even when shaken, waits a day for late wages and will come with you in the hunt ("Stay close to me"). Trust grows with fair pay and honest thanks; it wears when you underpay or wave away what they saw.
- **Calling Biscuit:** press the call key (B by default) and she comes to you, inside the camp fence. She will not follow you out of the fence and never comes out of the camper after dark. One of the things that walks at night never calls while she is beside you.
- **Get to know the first five.** Marta, Dale, Priya, Tomas and Joan each have three things to tell you over the contract. When someone is ready (enough days, enough trust) a new question appears when you talk to them. Each answer builds trust; after the third they are settled and content with a little less pay. Priya's notes help your Field notes along. A letter may come for them on the next boat (Journal → Crew).
- **When someone leaves,** the others notice. For a few days each of them has something to say about it, by name, the next time you talk.
- **Evenings at the fire:** at dusk, once the crew is back and if the fire is burning, use the stump by the burn pit to sit with them. Somebody tells a story; everyone there calms down a little and trusts you a little more. Once an evening.
- **Recordings:** when a camera caught something, talk to a worker and choose "Look at this recording." Seeing it moves them far more than being told.
- **Someone may not come home.** Once the island has started to stir, a sceptic can stay out past the fence one evening, listening to something. Take a work lamp, find them and say "Come back with me", or sleep: they walk back in at first light, shaken, and sit the morning out. Nobody is harmed.

## Nights (during the contract)

- A workday is 20 minutes of daylight and 5 minutes of dusk. Warnings come at the start of dusk, halfway and one minute before night.
- **Safe at night:** the camper (always, even with the door open), an occupied cabin, the pools of the working camp lamps, and the pool of a battery work lamp you have set down. Moonlight, flashlights and screens don't count.
- **Work lamps:** the company sends two with each lot. They are in the yellow lamp locker beside the parcel locker. Take one, carry it out and set it down with the set-down key. At night its light is a safe place for about seven minutes; it flickers for the last minute and then goes dark. Lamps recharge in daylight. You can't carry logs or swing an axe with a lamp in hand: set it down and work beside it.
- **Night shift at the sawmill:** the red switch on the sawmill's status board post starts a night shift. The saw then takes logs from its rack while you stay within about 14 m, and everything it cuts that night pays 50% more. It stops when you leave and ends at dawn. The mill is not a safe place, so set a work lamp down there first. The saw is loud: more of the night comes to look while it runs.
- **The breaker:** once the island is disturbed, the camp's pole lamps can flicker and go out during the night, and their pools stop being safe. Reset the breaker on the post beside the lockers (its light turns from red to green), or get inside the camper. Work lamps keep working.
- Night only ends when you sleep. Waiting doesn't bring morning. Staying out at night can end the attempt; you can reload that morning's checkpoint.
- **The nights grow.** An untouched island is quiet: on the first nights you only hear things. Each day, and above all each tree cut, wakes more of what lives here, and cleared ground is where it is worst. Late in a campaign the dark also closes in faster.
- **Everything out there keeps to one rule.** Some leave if you keep looking at them, some if you look away, some if you stand still, walk at them, walk away, get under a roof, or step into a real camp light. A few ask for nothing. Do what works and it goes quietly, leaves you alone for the rest of the night and buys you time outside. Do the wrong thing and it closes in.
- **Journal → Field notes** (the last tab) writes each thing down when you meet it, adds a hint after a few meetings, and states what works once you have seen it work twice. It also says how the island feels tonight: quiet, uneasy, disturbed or hostile.
- No rule makes you safe. Only the camper, an occupied cabin and the working lamps do.

## After the contract: the hunt

The hunt follows every contract. You get a rules card first (the game is paused until you continue). Something physical hunts you; lamps and the camper no longer guarantee safety. Hide, break line of sight and move quietly. Biscuit waits safely in the dock building and boards any ship on her own.

- **If you finished the contract:** survive three days. The company ship reaches the dock at dawn, and boarding stays open for 8 minutes. Your end summary includes the finished job's figures.
- **The crew radio (T):** call the roll, tell everyone to get out of sight, or ask them to come to you (only those who trust you will). Every call makes noise: your voice carries about 16 m and each spoken answer about 10 m. Anyone already hidden answers with two clicks and no sound. The radio never tells you why someone does not answer.
- **If you did not:** no ship is coming, and the thing hunting you guesses better the longer you last. The only way off is the emergency beacon in the dock building, which is dead.
  1. Check the beacon. It lists the three missing parts and where the spares are kept: two far field sheds and the sawmill control box.
  2. Fetch them. Taking each one makes noise.
  3. Fit them at the beacon and switch it on. It is loud, and it draws the hunter to the dock until the ship arrives at the next dawn.
  4. Board within 4 minutes.
- **If you miss a ship:** it leaves, and you are in the second case. The beacon has to be used (or used again).

## What pauses

The pause menu, Settings, Help, the Journal and management screens, rule cards and the arcade games pause everything. The couch and TV, talking, hiding and driving are live. See [TIME_AND_STATE.md](TIME_AND_STATE.md).

## Testing a build quickly

1. New Campaign → follow the guide through the first delivery (about 10 minutes).
2. Settings → rebind Interact, then check the on-screen prompts change.
3. Preview Tour (Tab) → Phase 6 stops → "Abandon -> hunt" for the hunt rules card; "Hunt: ship" for boarding.
4. Journal → Contract → "Locate the nearest remaining work".
